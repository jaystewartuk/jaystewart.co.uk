Case study

WantToJoin

One link per hostel: tonight's outing, who's going, and where the group was. Built with the hosts at one hostel. Closed on 14 October 2026 after a one-week pilot.

Role
Research, product, design and engineering
Status
Closed One-week pilot at one hostel, ended 14 October 2026
Time
Research to a live pilot in three days
Stack
Cloudflare Workers and D1, PostHog, Claude Haiku

What 2,800 messages showed

Before any code, I read five months of a hostel's guest WhatsApp group.

  • Hosts posted each evening's outing several times a day.
  • Polls got three votes at most.
  • "Where are you?" came up three or four times a week.
  • Tour prices were hidden inside pictures.

So: build for the hosts, not the guests. Hostelworld and SocialStay already do RSVPs, so I built only what they don't: nothing to download, a live meeting pin, and a minimum headcount with a deadline.

Decisions from the evidence

  • Take input as it arrives. The rota came as a photo of the reception chalkboard, so the week view takes those lines.
  • Change the metric when corrected. A host said posting several times a day is her job. The measure became "every post carries the link".
  • Follow the money. A tour promoted for 22 days with no price sold within 15 minutes once a guest got the price. Tours showed price and time.
  • Write down what not to build. No accounts, chat or payments in version one.

AI where it pays

Hosts wrote every post twice, in English and Spanish. "Write it for me" drafted both from the form; the host edited before saving.

  • Code checked times, day, place and price. One retry, then the host is told what to check.
  • Evals: 50 of 50 drafts pass, 92% first time, 2.2 s median, about $0.0003 a post.
  • The evals caught two bugs in my own checker. Both fixed and tested.

How it was built

Cloudflare Workers and D1 on the free plan, server-rendered, installable. Every change got a pull request, tests and a phone-width check in both languages. A merge deployed, checked the live site and rolled back if that failed. Analytics never sent guest names.

Close-out

Day 1: every host post carried the link (4 of 4), and guests opened the page each time. Nobody tapped "I'm in". It rained, the hostel was nearly empty, and neither outing happened.

The pilot ran for a week and I closed it on 14 October 2026. Final numbers, from the database:

  • 65 page opens between 6 and 14 October. That counts opens, not people. 32 came from the link in WhatsApp posts and 9 from the QR code.
  • 12 outings were posted on the page.
  • 3 RSVPs, from three devices, for two of those outings (12 and 13 October). The other ten had none.
  • 6 AI drafts from "Write it for me", 4 of them saved.

Three RSVPs is too few to compare with turnout, so the question the pilot was for stayed open: do RSVPs match who turns up? I did not reach a second hostel. I closed it to spend my time on finding a role. The site is down and the code is private.
